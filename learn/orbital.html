3D Orbital Explorer

Real hydrogen orbitals in GPU 3D, with a neural network that learns the wavefunction live.

1. Principle

Idea. An electron in an atom isn't a dot on an orbit — it's a cloud of probability. Solving the Schrödinger equation for hydrogen gives these clouds, the orbitals ψₙₗₘ. Each dot here is a place the electron is likely to be; blue and red mark where the wavefunction is positive or negative. Where the colour vanishes is a node.

2. Key formulas

ψₙₗₘ(r,θ,φ) = Rₙₗ(r)·Yₗₘ(θ,φ) · Eₙ = −13.6 / n² eV
  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. Raising n adds shells; raising ℓ adds lobes. Nodes = n − 1 in total.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture (particles, waves, fields, or levels) described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: ψₙₗₘ(r,θ,φ) = Rₙₗ(r)·Yₗₘ(θ,φ) · Eₙ = −13.6 / n² eV
Step C. Change one control at a time in the experiment and predict the sign of the change before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
