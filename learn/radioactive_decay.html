Experiment guide

Radioactive Decay Simulator

Choose an isotope (C-14…), set N₀ up to 10,000, and watch probabilistic decay — green bars vs the ideal exponential law.

1. Principle

Idea. Radioactivity is random for each nucleus but predictable for a large sample. The number of undecayed nuclei falls exponentially. After one half-life, about half remain; after two, about a quarter — with random statistical variation around that average.

2. Key formulas

N(t)=N_0 e^{-\lambda t}\quad\cdot\quad T_{1/2}=\ln 2/\lambda\quad\cdot\quad A=\lambda N
  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. Try Carbon-14 with N₀ = 10,000. Green bars = this random sample; blue curve = ideal law. Markers show 1×, 2×, 3× half-life. Reset to see a new noisy path.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture (particles, waves, fields, or levels) described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: N(t)=N_0 e^{-\lambda t}\quad\cdot\quad T_{1/2}=\ln 2/\lambda\quad\cdot\quad A=\lambda N
Step C. Change one control at a time in the experiment and predict the sign of the change before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
