Experiment guide

³He & BF₃ Proportional Counters

Detect neutrons via capture charged products; thermal ≫ fast efficiency; optional moderator and wall effect.

1. Principle

Idea. Neutrons are neutral and leave little direct ionization. Gas proportional counters detect them through capture reactions that yield charged particles: ³He(n,p)³H or ¹⁰B(n,α)⁷Li. Thermal neutrons are detected far more efficiently than fast ones; a moderator helps thermalize a fast beam.

2. Key formulas

^3\mathrm{He}(n,p)^3\mathrm{H}\ (Q\approx0.76\,\mathrm{MeV})\quad\cdot\quad ^{10}\mathrm{B}(n,\alpha)^7\mathrm{Li}
  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. Efficiency curve peaks at thermal. Pulse-height peak near Q; continuum = wall effect.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture (particles, waves, fields, or levels) described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: ^3\mathrm{He}(n,p)^3\mathrm{H}\ (Q\approx0.76\,\mathrm{MeV})\quad\cdot\quad ^{10}\mathrm{B}(n,\alpha)^7\mathrm{Li}
Step C. Change one control at a time in the experiment and predict the sign of the change before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
