Experiment guide

Si Detector α Spectroscopy

Discrete α lines (Am-241, Pu-239, …) with excellent vacuum resolution; air path shifts and smears peaks.

1. Principle

Idea. Silicon charged-particle detectors measure α energies with excellent resolution in vacuum. In air, α particles lose energy (dE/dx) and straggle — peaks shift to lower energy and broaden. Always run α spectroscopy in vacuum (or with a tiny source–detector gap).

2. Key formulas

E_{\mathrm{obs}} \approx E_0 - \tfrac{dE}{dx}\,x \quad\cdot\quad \mathrm{FWHM}\uparrow\ \text{with air}
  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. Green dashed = vacuum line energies. With air, gold spectrum shifts left and widens.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture (particles, waves, fields, or levels) described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: E_{\mathrm{obs}} \approx E_0 - \tfrac{dE}{dx}\,x \quad\cdot\quad \mathrm{FWHM}\uparrow\ \text{with air}
Step C. Change one control at a time in the experiment and predict the sign of the change before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
