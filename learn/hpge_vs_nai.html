HPGe vs NaI Resolution

Overlay MCA spectra for the same γ source — sharp HPGe peaks (~0.2%) vs broad NaI (~7%), FWHM and R = FWHM/E.

1. Principle

Idea. Energy resolution R = FWHM/E. NaI(Tl) scintillators (~6–8% at 662 keV) are cheap and room-temperature. High-purity germanium (~0.2%) resolves close lines but needs cryogenic cooling to keep leakage noise low.

2. Key formulas

R = \mathrm{FWHM}/E \quad\cdot\quad \mathrm{FWHM} \propto \sqrt{E}\ \text{(teaching)}
  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. Cyan = NaI spectrum · green = HPGe. Co-60: HPGe shows two clean peaks; NaI may blend them.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture (particles, waves, fields, or levels) described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: R = \mathrm{FWHM}/E \quad\cdot\quad \mathrm{FWHM} \propto \sqrt{E}\ \text{(teaching)}
Step C. Change one control at a time in the experiment and predict the sign of the change before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
