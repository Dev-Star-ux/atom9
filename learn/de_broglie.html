Experiment guide

de Broglie Matter Waves

Every moving particle has wavelength λ = h/p. Plot λ vs speed or energy (MatterWaveX-style), watch the matter wave, and let an AI discover h.

1. Principle

Idea. de Broglie (1924): every particle with momentum p has wavelength λ = h/p. Electrons at eV energies have atomic-sized λ — the basis of diffraction and electron microscopy. This plotter mirrors MatterWaveX: choose particle, velocity or kinetic energy, non-relativistic or relativistic.

2. Key formulas

\lambda=\frac{h}{p}\quad\cdot\quad p=mv\;(\text{non-rel})
  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. 2D: traveling matter wave + λ(v) or λ(E) curve (green marker = now). 3D: particle rides a glowing wave ribbon; yellow ticks mark one λ. Switch Electron → Fullerene C₆₀ — λ collapses.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture (particles, waves, fields, or levels) described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: \lambda=\frac{h}{p}\quad\cdot\quad p=mv\;(\text{non-rel})
Step C. Change one control at a time in the experiment and predict the sign of the change before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
