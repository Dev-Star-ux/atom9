Electrostatic Precipitator

Dirty gas flows through a duct past a high-voltage corona wire and grounded collector plates: dust gets charged, drifts sideways, and sticks — cleaning the exhaust before it reaches the stack.

1. Principle

Idea. Industrial flue gas from power plants, cement kilns, and smelters carries fine ash and dust. An electrostatic precipitator (ESP) removes it in three steps: (1) a thin wire at high voltage sustains a corona discharge, flooding the gas with ions; (2) passing dust particles are bombarded by these ions and pick up a charge roughly proportional to their surface area (∝ r²); (3) the strong electric field between the wire and the grounded plates pushes the charged particles sideways until they drift onto a plate and stick, while cleaned gas exits downstream. Periodically the plates are "rapped" (vibrated) to shake the collected dust into a hopper below.

2. Key formulas

E \approx V/d\quad\cdot\quad w = \dfrac{qE}{6\pi\mu r}\quad\cdot\quad \eta = 1 - e^{-wA/Q}
  • E: field between wire and plate (V/m); V: applied voltage; d: wire-to-plate spacing.
  • w: drift (migration) velocity, from balancing the electric force qE against Stokes drag 6πμrv.
  • η: Deutsch-Anderson collection efficiency; A: total plate area; Q: volumetric gas flow rate.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. Dust dots enter on the left, drift with the gas, and curve toward whichever plate is nearer — the curvature strength tracks the drift velocity w. Particles that reach a plate pile up there; the gas thins out (fewer dots) toward the exit as more dust is captured. The bottom plot shows collection efficiency η rising with particle radius r, with a marker at the current operating point.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture: corona charging, electric drift, and plate collection, described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: E \approx V/d\quad\cdot\quad w = \dfrac{qE}{6\pi\mu r}\quad\cdot\quad \eta = 1 - e^{-wA/Q}
Step C. Change one control at a time (voltage, particle size, gas velocity, plate spacing) and predict the sign of the change in η before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
