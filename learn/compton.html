Compton Effect — Photon Momentum

X-rays scatter off electrons with a wavelength shift Δλ = λ_C(1−cos θ). An AI measures the Compton wavelength λ_C from shift data.

1. Principle

Idea. When an X-ray photon collides with a nearly free electron, the scattered photon emerges at angle θ with a longer wavelength. The shift Δλ = (h/mc)(1−cos θ) depends only on θ — proving photons carry momentum, not just energy.

2. Key formulas

\Delta\lambda=\lambda_C(1-\cos\theta)\quad\cdot\quad \lambda_C=\frac{h}{m_e c}
  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. Larger scattering angle → bigger Δλ. Shift is independent of initial wavelength.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture (particles, waves, fields, or levels) described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: \Delta\lambda=\lambda_C(1-\cos\theta)\quad\cdot\quad \lambda_C=\frac{h}{m_e c}
Step C. Change one control at a time in the experiment and predict the sign of the change before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
