Davisson–Germer — Electron Diffraction

Electrons scatter from a Ni crystal with intensity peaks at Bragg angles. λ = h/√(2meV) — let an AI discover crystal spacing d.

1. Principle

Idea. Davisson and Germer (1927) fired low-energy electrons at a nickel crystal and observed diffraction peaks — proof that electrons are matter waves. Intensity spikes when nλ = D sin θ (surface grating), matching de Broglie λ = h/√(2meV).

2. Key formulas

λ = h/√(2meV) · nλ = D sin θ
  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. 2D: apparatus + I(θ) curve — green marker is your detector. 3D: watch electrons scatter; detector glows at a Bragg peak. At V ≈ 54 V the first peak is near θ ≈ 50° (Ni D ≈ 218 pm).

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture (particles, waves, fields, or levels) described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: λ = h/√(2meV) · nλ = D sin θ
Step C. Change one control at a time in the experiment and predict the sign of the change before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
