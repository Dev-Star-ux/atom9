Corona Discharge

A localized, self-sustained ionization glow that forms right at a sharp point, thin wire, or sharp edge when the LOCAL field there exceeds threshold — even while the average field across the whole gap stays far below full-spark breakdown.

1. Principle

Idea. Charge concentrates on regions of small radius of curvature — the sharper the point, the higher the local field for a given voltage. Near a fine needle or thin wire, the field can locally exceed the ionization threshold of air while the bulk of the gap remains far below breakdown. The result is a small halo of ionization confined to the tip, not a glow filling the gap and not a full arc. This is the SAME field-concentration idea used by lightning rods (which deliberately raise a small, controlled corona to bleed off charge) and by dust precipitators (which use a sharp emitting wire to ionize particles).

2. Key formulas

E_{onset} = 30\,\delta\left(1+\dfrac{0.301}{\sqrt{\delta r}}\right)

Peek's law: the onset field (kV/cm) at the surface of a conductor of radius r (cm) in air of relative density δ (δ = 1.0 at standard temperature/pressure; δ falls with altitude or with rising temperature). The actual field at the tip of a needle of radius r at voltage V (kV) with gap d (cm) to a grounded plane is approximated by E_tip ≈ V / [r·ln(2d/r)]. Corona ignites once E_tip ≥ E_onset.

  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. A violet-blue halo with short branching streamer filaments appears only at the needle tip once E_tip crosses E_onset — the plane electrode and the rest of the gap stay dark. In the bottom plot, watch the rising E_tip(V) curve cross the flat E_onset line; that crossing is the corona onset voltage.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture: field lines crowd together near a small-radius conductor, concentrating the field there far above the average field in the gap.
Step B. Write the governing relation: E_{onset} = 30\delta(1+0.301/\sqrt{\delta r})
Step C. Change one control at a time — shrink r, raise V, change δ (altitude) — and predict whether corona turns on before you look.
Step D. Compare with real effects: power-line corona losses and audible hissing, the ozone smell near high-voltage equipment, and St. Elmo's fire on ship masts and aircraft wingtips in storms — all are the same localized point-discharge phenomenon.
