GM Tube: Energy Response & Dead Time

Why a Geiger counter cannot measure energy, how dead time loses counts at high rate, and the HV plateau.

1. Principle

Idea. In the Geiger region the avalanche saturates, so pulse height is independent of deposited energy — you get counts, not a spectrum. After each count the tube is “dead” for time τ; at high true rates m many events are missed. Non-paralyzable model: n = m/(1+mτ).

2. Key formulas

n = \frac{m}{1+m\tau}\quad\cdot\quad m = \frac{n}{1-n\tau}
  • Identify each symbol in the formula and its unit.
  • Ask which quantity you control in the simulator, and which you measure.

3. What to observe

In the sim. Green pulses = counted · red = lost in τ. Gold dot = your (m,n) on the curve. Energy view: flat bars vs proportional rising bars.

4. Step-by-step reasoning

Step A. Start from the physical picture (particles, waves, fields, or levels) described above.
Step B. Write the governing relation: n = \frac{m}{1+m\tau}\quad\cdot\quad m = \frac{n}{1-n\tau}
Step C. Change one control at a time in the experiment and predict the sign of the change before you look.
Step D. When an AI panel is present, collect data and compare the learned constant with the accepted value.
